## The problem

A catalog of roughly **7,000 SKUs** across Etsy, Amazon, Walmart, and Shopify,
built up over years by different people at different times. Traffic wasn't the
issue — the listings were getting seen. They just weren't closing.

Conversion sat around **3%**. On a catalog this size, that's not a listing
problem, it's a systems problem: no consistent photography standard, keyword
sets that were guesses rather than research, titles written for the internal
team instead of the buyer, and descriptions that made a shopper work to answer
a basic question before they'd commit.

## The approach

There was no single fix here, and I want to be honest about that — this was
three years of grinding through a catalog, not one clever campaign. The work
broke into four passes, run continuously rather than in sequence:

- **Photography.** Every listing image judged on one question: does this
  answer what the buyer is actually asking? Shot and retouched in-house, so
  iterating cost time instead of money.
- **Keywords.** Real research replacing inherited guesses — eRank for the Etsy
  side, Ahrefs for search demand more broadly. The term map got rebuilt around
  what people search, not what we call things internally.
- **Titles and descriptions.** Rewritten to lead with the buying decision.
  Front-load what it is, who it's for, and the objection most likely to stop
  the sale.
- **Ease of purchase.** The unglamorous one, and probably the highest-leverage:
  removing every point where a shopper has to pause, re-read, or go hunting for
  information before they can commit. Variant structure, sizing clarity,
  shipping expectations, the lot.

## The result

Conversion across the storefronts moved from **3% to 6%** — a doubling, held
over three years and across a catalog that kept growing.

<blockquote>
Doubling conversion means every dollar already being spent to get someone to
the page suddenly works twice as hard. Nothing about the traffic changed.
</blockquote>

The compounding matters more than the headline. At this catalog's scale, a
conversion point isn't a one-time win — it applies to every session, on every
listing, every day after. It's the single change I'd point to as having moved
the business most.

Alongside it, the operational side held up: a **4.9 rating across three Etsy
shops**, a **99% on-time shipping rate**, and a 5/5 response record on customer
messages. Conversion work only counts if the fulfillment behind it doesn't
crack under the extra volume.
